# Support and SLAs

## What the platform already tells you

Before an agreement is involved, several things are observable rather than promised.

**Limits are published.** Payload cap, batch ceilings and every rate bucket are on one page, with the numbers — see [Limits](/locusgraph/client/limits). You can design against them without asking anyone.

**Failures are legible.** Every call throws on failure with the status and the server's body, and the split is clean: `4xx` means the request was wrong, `429` and `5xx` mean the moment was wrong. Only the second group is worth retrying. See [Errors](/locusgraph/client/errors).

**Model-backed calls report their own cost.** [Deep recall](/locusgraph/recall/deep-recall) returns `usage` on every call — LLM calls, tokens both ways, and dollars — and a `coverage` field saying whether the walk finished. You are not inferring spend from a monthly invoice.

**Degraded answers announce themselves.** `coverage: "partial"` and `fallback: true` say the answer came from less than a full walk. A system that returned the same shape either way would be harder to operate.

## Rate limits by bucket

| Bucket | Limit | What is in it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `spend` | 20/min | Search, deep recall |
| `ingest` | 10/min | Document ingestion |
| `write` | 120/min | Writes, batches, transactions, observations |
| `read` | 300/min | Everything else |

If these ceilings do not fit your volume, that is a conversation rather than a blocker.

## What is not on this page

Uptime commitments, response times, escalation paths, dedicated capacity and contractual terms are not published here. They are agreed, not documented — and a docs page claiming an SLA that your contract does not say is worse than no page.

## Reporting something broken

Include the thrown message verbatim. It carries the status and the server's body:

```
Request failed: 429 Too Many Requests - {"error":"rate limit exceeded"}
```

For a deep recall that answered badly, include `coverage`, `rounds_used`, `fallback` and `contexts_walked`. Those four say more about what happened than the summary does.
