# A memory that outlives the conversation

Your assistant learns that Alice prefers dark mode. Three days later she asks it to build a settings page, and it ships one in white.

Nothing broke. The model did exactly what models do — it read the messages in front of it and answered. The problem is everything that was not in front of it.

The usual patch is to stuff more into the prompt. That works until the history is longer than the window, and then you are choosing what to forget on every single call, with no idea what any of it was worth.

## What LocusGraph is

A store your app writes to when it learns something, and reads from when it needs to remember.

What goes in is **experience** — one thing that happened, stated plainly, first-hand. Not a
summary of a conversation. Not a chunk of a document. Not the model's own output folded back in.

<ExperienceVsConclusion />

The left column is what happened. The right is what something *made of* what happened — and once
you store that, you have stored an opinion with no way back to the evidence. Three months later
nobody can tell whether "design-conscious" came from one offhand remark or twenty.

Keep the memory pure and the conclusions stay yours to draw, fresh, with the evidence still
attached. A model reading five plain memories will infer the pattern when it needs it; a model
reading last quarter's inference cannot recover the five.

<MemoryFlow />

You never write the query that finds it. You ask in the words you have, and memories that *mean* the same thing come back — even when they share no words with the question.

## What makes it a graph

Memories are not a flat pile. Each one is filed under a **context** — a stable name like `preference:dark_mode` or `user:alice` — and contexts connect to each other.

<ContextGraph />

So a question about Alice can reach what she prefers, and a question about the billing UI can reach who asked for it. One context holds many memories; one memory can sit under several contexts.

<Callout tone="note" title="Contexts are the handle, memories are the content">
  You **store and search memories**. You **address them by context**. Keep a context slug short and canonical — the same idea should always land in the same place, or you have two half-filled drawers instead of one full one.
</Callout>
